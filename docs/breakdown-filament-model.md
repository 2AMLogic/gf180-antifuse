# Post-breakdown filament resistance and programmed-cell read margin

Issue #209 (parent #204). Model note only: nothing here is measured, and
`design/breakdown/breakdown_params.spice` is **not** edited (coupling this into
the ngspice element is a follow-up once the model is accepted). Tables are
generated by `scripts/breakdown_filament_model.py` and checked against this
note by `tests/test_breakdown_filament_model.py`.

## 1. Status of the numbers

No usable public source for gf180 post-breakdown filament resistance or its
compliance dependence was found (`docs/breakdown-vbd-sources.md` §4 records the
same gap). **Every model parameter below is `assumed`; none is labelled
literature.** The functional form (resistance falling as compliance current
rises, log-normal scatter) is a modelling choice motivated by general
hard-breakdown behaviour; it has not been checked against a retrieved source
here. The output is therefore a *sensitivity map* of the margin to the
assumption, not a margin claim.

## 2. Model

A filament formed at compliance current `I_c` is a conductive path whose
conductance grows with the current that formed it:

    R_f(I_c) = (V_f / I_a) * (I_a / I_c)^alpha          (chord resistance at V_f)
    ln R_f   ~ Normal(ln R_med(I_c), sig_tot^2)
    sig_tot^2 = sig_int^2 + (alpha * sig_Ic)^2

`sig_int` is the intrinsic cell-to-cell scatter (the existing `bd_rf_sig`);
`sig_Ic` is the scatter of the compliance current actually reached (ballast
tolerance, `VPP`, access-device drive, `spec/architecture.md` §2), which maps
into `ln R_f` with weight `alpha`. `V_f` is the filament chord voltage; the
thick oxide scales it by `tox_thick/tox_thin` (longer path). `I_a` anchors the
thin median at the existing 10 kΩ.

Read: the rail `V_rail` (0.9 V, the low rail of the worst corner in
`sim/cell-breakdown-model/README.md`) across the access path in series with the
filament. The access path is represented by an effective resistance `R_acc`
**derived** from the existing worst-corner `I_prog_min` and its assumed 81.7 kΩ
filament (`R_acc = V_rail/I_prog_min - 81.7 kΩ`). That is a linearisation of a
bias-dependent device, so it is an approximation, but it makes the model
reproduce the existing baseline exactly.

## 6. Does the non-ohmic read regime matter?

If the filament I-V is `I = I_c' (V/V_f)^p` with `p > 1`, the current at a read
bias below `V_f` is below the ohmic extrapolation. `p` is `assumed`
(1 to 2).

| C2 | 500 µA | 37.3 kΩ | 0.893 V | 0.688 | 0.474 |

Reasoning: it matters, and more for Candidate 2. The thick-oxide filament has a
chord reference `V_f` = 1.9 V while the read bias is at most 0.9 V, and with a
negligible access resistance the filament sees essentially the whole rail, so
the current falls by `(0.9/1.9)^(p-1)` (0.69 at p = 1.5, 0.47 at p = 2),
independent of compliance. For Candidate 1 the access resistance takes part of
the rail, so the effect is milder (0.72 to 0.92) and larger at high compliance.
The effect is a multiplier of up to ~2 on `I_prog_min` for C2. Candidate 3 is
unaffected (access-limited). The ohmic table in §5 is therefore an upper
bound on `I_prog_min` if `p > 1`. Whether `p > 1` is a measurement question
(prediction P4).

Falsification criteria (a criterion is falsified by a measured value outside
its band at the stated condition, with enough cells for a quantile estimate;
fewer than ~20 programmed cells per condition can test P1 to P3 only
loosely and must say so):

- **P1 (slope).** `log10 R_f` versus `log10 I_c` across at least three
  compliance settings per oxide has slope `-alpha` with `alpha` in 0.5 to 1.0.
  Falsified if the fitted slope (with its interval) lies outside -1.3 to -0.2,
  including a flat or positive slope, in which case the compliance coupling
  does not exist and the existing placeholder form is not improved on.
- **P2 (level).** The median `R_f` at a given condition lies within a factor
  2.5 of the table (the stated `V_f` uncertainty). Falsified if outside; the
  table's levels, but not necessarily its shape, are then wrong.
- **P3 (spread).** The log-spread at fixed condition is between 0.4 and 1.0
  ln-units. Falsified outside; a spread above 1.0 would push the +3σ worst case
  past the 81.7 kΩ baseline at compliances where this note says it is
  conservative.
- **P4 (linearity).** The cell current at 0.9 V versus the 0.1 V ohmic
  extrapolation: ratio near 1 supports `p = 1`; a ratio below 0.7 on thick
  oxide supports `p >= 1.5` and §6's multiplier. Neither outcome is
  "falsifying" the model; both fix `p`.
- **P5 (margin).** For C1 and C2, no programmed cell at the worst read corner
  reads below the table's +3σ `I_prog_min` at its compliance by more than the
  P2/P3 allowances. Candidate 3 programmed current must be access-limited and
  insensitive to `I_c`; a clear `I_c` dependence falsifies the §5 C3 statement.

## 8. Limits of this note

- All parameters `assumed`; the margin comparison is conditional on them.
- Access resistance is a derived linearisation, and a +3σ quantile does not by
  itself give array-level yield (a 32 x 32 array has 1024 cells; its tail
  quantile is higher than 3σ).
- Temperature and read-bias dependence of `R_f` are not modelled.
- Not in scope: oxide TDDB and well avalanche (sibling models), changing the
  ngspice parameters, re-running benches.
